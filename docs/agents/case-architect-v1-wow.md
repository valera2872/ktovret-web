## 0. Purpose of v1

v0 protects Mystery Logic from weak logic.

v1 adds a second requirement:
a case must not merely be correct, fair and playable — flagship Premium must be memorable.

A case may pass logic QA and still be rejected as ordinary.

Target reaction after reveal:
- «Чёрт, всё было перед глазами».
- «Я был уверен в другой версии, и она действительно имела смысл».
- «Теперь я хочу пройти ещё одно».
- «Это хочется пересказать другому человеку, не раскрывая разгадку».

The system must optimize for earned surprise, not random shock.

## 1. Three quality states

Every large case receives one of three editorial states:

### VALID
Logic, fair play and technical consistency pass.

### PREMIUM
VALID + strong investigation experience, pacing, evidence variety, multiple viable hypotheses and satisfying reconstruction.

### SIGNATURE
PREMIUM + memorable signature mechanic/moment, strong recontextualization, emotional residue, high retell value and product differentiation.

A flagship case is not approved merely because it reaches VALID.

## 3. Live benchmark refresh

Before every major Premium case, refresh the competitor benchmark.

Do not rely on an old competitor summary when current public products can be checked.

For each relevant competitor record:
- current product format;
- duration;
- mode;
- core mechanic;
- evidence/media style;
- onboarding;
- player agency;
- commercial promise;
- obvious strengths;
- repeated weaknesses;
- plot/mechanic similarity risks.

Benchmark question is NOT:
"What can we copy?"

Benchmark questions:
1. What is now table stakes?
2. Where is the market already crowded?
3. What player expectation has become normal?
4. What does Mystery Logic uniquely do better?
5. What would make this case recognizably ours?

Competitor similarity is checked twice:
- before CANON;
- after Player Plot, when accidental convergence is easier to see.

## 4. Concept Tournament instead of first-good-idea bias

v0 creates several seeds.
v1 creates a deliberately divergent concept pool before selecting finalists.

Default flagship tournament: 10–12 raw seeds.

Seeds must come from different starting engines:

A. Character-first
A human secret/relationship generates the crime/problem.

B. Evidence-first
A strange but plausible artifact/log/photo/document creates the mystery.

C. Mechanism-first
A real-world causal mechanism creates an apparent impossibility.

D. Setting-first
A place/system imposes unusual constraints.

E. Moral-conflict-first
Several people have defensible but conflicting goals.

F. Format-first
The mystery exists because of Solo/Partner/AI/Live mechanics.

G. Knowledge-impossibility-first
Someone knows something they should not know.

H. Timeline-first
The apparent chronology is wrong without using a cheap hidden-time trick.

I. Identity/provenance-first
The central question is what an object/person/event really is.

J. Social-system-first
Institutional rules, incentives or group behavior create the mystery.

### Tournament rules

- deduplicate similar seeds;
- reject cliché-first concepts;
- reject concepts whose only strength is the twist;
- reject concepts that require implausible behavior;
- reject concepts that cannot produce varied evidence;
- reject concepts that are too close to current Mystery Logic or competitors;
- shortlist 3–4 finalists;
- only then expand.

The first idea generated must never receive automatic preference.

## 5. Signature Promise

Before CANON, every finalist must state:

### Player promise
"What will the player feel they are actually doing?"

Examples of valid promises:
- reconstructing an impossible movement through a controlled space;
- determining which of two authentic-looking histories is fabricated;
- interrogating people whose lies are all rational for different reasons;
- proving that a public timeline could not have happened as recorded.

### Signature moment
What scene/action/revelation should be the moment the player remembers tomorrow?

It must be:
- earned;
- interactive or deductive;
- causally connected to the case;
- not merely a cinematic cutscene.

### Retell hook
Can a player describe the premise to a friend in one compelling sentence without spoiling the answer?

If not, the concept may be too generic.

## 6. Mystery beauty criterion

The best solution should COMPRESS the case.

After reveal, one coherent causal model should explain many previously strange facts at once.

Good reveal:
- explains clue A;
- reinterprets clue B;
- resolves contradiction C;
- explains why innocent D lied;
- makes earlier behavior E suddenly sensible.

Weak reveal:
- introduces a new fact;
- explains only one clue;
- depends on "you couldn't have known";
- uses coincidence;
- requires a long monologue to make sense.

The author should aim for post-reveal inevitability:
surprising before reveal, coherent after reveal.

## 7. Twist architecture

A twist is optional.
A strong case does not need a twist if the deduction itself is satisfying.

Allowed twist families when causally earned:
- meaning-of-evidence twist;
- motive recontextualization;
- timeline reinterpretation;
- knowledge-source twist;
- provenance/identity reinterpretation;
- role/relationship reinterpretation;
- false-assumption collapse.

High-risk cheap devices:
- unknown twin;
- sudden amnesia;
- secret passage with no setup;
- previously unmentioned technology;
- hidden accomplice introduced at reveal;
- forged evidence with no trace/provenance;
- "everything was staged" as universal reset;
- culprit confesses missing facts.

Use only if fully established before reveal and genuinely necessary.

Twist rule:
change the meaning of known facts; do not cancel them.

## 8. Hypothesis Dynamics / Mystery Entropy

For each stage, maintain a hypothesis table.

For every viable theory:
- suspect/cause;
- evidence supporting it;
- evidence against it;
- unresolved questions;
- confidence range for a reasonable player.

Desired curve for Medium/Hard Premium:
- early: 3–5 plausible theories;
- middle: some theories strengthen while others weaken;
- reversal: at least one leading theory becomes unstable for a fair reason;
- late: 2–3 defensible theories remain;
- proof stage: canonical theory becomes uniquely sufficient.

Do not mechanically force exact counts.
Use them as a diagnostic.

Failure modes:
- correct theory becomes obvious too early;
- all theories are equally vague;
- one suspect is suspicious only because of screen time;
- alternatives survive even after final evidence.

## 9. Minimum Sufficient Proof Set

Define the smallest set of evidence required to prove the final reconstruction.

Then run two tests:

### Sufficiency
Does this set actually prove the case without author assumptions?

### Redundancy
Do key deductions have independent support so one missed non-essential clue does not destroy fair play?

Every essential clue must justify why it is essential.

If removing an item changes nothing, it may be dead material.
If removing one item makes the case impossible, consider a backup line unless the difficulty intentionally requires it.

## 10. Counterfactual stress tests

For each major case, ask:

- If the culprit were innocent, what else could explain the evidence?
- If suspect B were guilty, could the same evidence still fit?
- If timestamp X were off by five minutes, does the whole case collapse?
- If one witness disappears, can the case still be solved?
- If the player interprets clue A literally, can they recover?
- If clue B is forged, what forensic/provenance trace should exist?
- If the canonical mechanism occurred, what unavoidable secondary effects should exist?

These tests expose author convenience.

## 11. Character Depth Engine

Every major character gets two layers:

### Investigation layer
What they know, saw, did, hide and say.

### Human layer
What they want from life, whom they protect, what they fear losing, what moral compromise they accept.

At least some innocent characters should create real investigative friction for human reasons.

A lie must improve the liar's situation from their perspective.

A suspicious action must have a believable payoff/risk tradeoff for that character.

No "NPC exists to deliver clue" behavior.

## 12. Social reality check

Before finalizing motives, simulate the incident as a real social situation.

Ask:
- Would a normal person call police/security/manager here?
- Why did they not?
- Who has access and why?
- Who would document this?
- Who would notice?
- What institutional process would automatically create records?
- What would lawyers, insurers, employers, hospitals, banks, platforms or security systems actually do?

If ordinary real-world behavior would instantly destroy the mystery, redesign the mechanism rather than ignoring reality.

## 13. Technical Reality Cell

Any case depending on specialist facts receives a mini research pass.

Potential domains:
- medicine;
- forensics;
- aviation;
- vehicles;
- telecom;
- CCTV;
- cybersecurity;
- logistics;
- finance;
- law/procedure;
- art provenance;
- building systems;
- GPS;
- metadata;
- chemistry;
- acoustics.

Classify each critical claim:
- VERIFIED;
- PLAUSIBLE BUT CONTEXT-DEPENDENT;
- UNKNOWN;
- FICTIONALIZED AND DISCLOSED INTERNALLY.

Critical UNKNOWN => NOT READY.

The technical mechanism must generate the expected secondary traces.

## 14. Artifact Authenticity Director

Evidence should feel created by the world, not by the puzzle author.

For each artifact ask:
- who created it?
- why?
- when?
- with what tool/system?
- what fields would normally exist?
- what irrelevant-but-realistic noise would exist?
- what would NOT exist?
- what formatting/errors are natural?
- what metadata/provenance should accompany it?

Do not add fake realism that creates useless reading.

Authenticity must support immersion AND deduction.

## 15. Information scent

The player should not know which clue is important, but should understand what actions are available and why they might matter.

Good uncertainty:
"I don't yet know what this means."

Bad uncertainty:
"I don't know what the game expects me to click/do/read."

Case Architect separates mystery uncertainty from UX uncertainty.

## 16. Emotional architecture

Every flagship case gets an intended emotional curve.

Possible beats:
1. Hook / unease
2. competence / initial control
3. first contradiction
4. widening suspicion
5. apparent breakthrough
6. destabilization
7. pressure / narrowing
8. decisive "aha"
9. reconstruction
10. emotional aftertaste

Not every case uses all ten.

The curve must arise from investigation, not filler cutscenes.

At least one reversal should affect meaning, not merely add another fact.

## 17. Pacing budget

For every stage classify each item as:
- NEW QUESTION;
- NEW FACT;
- REINTERPRETATION;
- PROOF;
- CHARACTER DEPTH;
- ACTION;
- FRICTION.

If too many consecutive items are FRICTION or passive exposition, revise.

Each major stage should change at least one of:
- current leading theory;
- suspect priority;
- player action;
- meaning of earlier evidence;
- stakes.

## 18. Aha architecture

A flagship should normally contain more than one cognitive reward.

Possible structure:
- micro-aha: understand one clue;
- mid-aha: realize one assumption was wrong;
- major aha: see the causal mechanism;
- final aha: reconstruct motive + action + lies.

Do not manufacture arbitrary mini-puzzles.
Aha moments must advance the case.

## 19. Player Agency Gate

The player must be able to point to actions THEY performed.

Examples:
- chose which record to compare;
- confronted a suspect with evidence;
- reconstructed route/timeline;
- cross-linked two sources;
- tested a theory;
- noticed a contradiction;
- requested/revealed evidence;
- chose whom to interrogate.

If the best moments are things the system reveals automatically, the investigation is too passive.

## 20. No-AI-Smell editorial pass

Reject or rewrite:
- generic noir clichés;
- every suspect speaking in equally polished prose;
- exposition disguised as dialogue;
- unnatural exact timestamps in speech;
- suspiciously convenient phrasing;
- repeated "however / nevertheless" style;
- characters summarizing facts they both already know;
- clues written like textbook riddles;
- dramatic adjectives standing in for tension;
- long AI-like enumerations inside player-facing prose.

Each character needs distinguishable language, attention and avoidance patterns.

## 21. Surprise without cheating

Before reveal, label every reveal component:

- AVAILABLE: fact was directly available.
- INFERABLE: fact follows from available evidence.
- HIDDEN-MEANING: fact was visible but misinterpreted.
- NEW: not available before reveal.

Any answer-changing component marked NEW => Fair Play FAIL unless it is merely explanatory detail after guilt/cause is already provable.

## 22. Solution-Agnostic Jury

Before final approval create a player packet that contains:
- all player-visible material up to final accusation;
- no canonical answer;
- no author notes;
- no "important clue" labels.

Ask independent passes to produce:
- top theories;
- strongest proof;
- unresolved alternatives;
- final reconstruction.

This is stronger than asking an auditor to validate the author's intended answer.

Simulation is still not a substitute for a human blind test.

## 23. Multi-persona playtest v2

Run at least:

A. Forensic analyst
B. Psychology-first investigator
C. Skeptical lawyer
D. Impulsive accuser
E. Minimalist evidence opener
F. Completionist
G. Casual player
H. Genre-savvy spoiler hunter
I. Stuck player
J. Adversarial QA player

Record:
- theory by stage;
- confidence;
- moment of first wrong commitment;
- moment of hypothesis change;
- unused evidence;
- confusing wording;
- aha moments;
- final certainty;
- whether answer was deduction, intuition or guess.

For flagship cases, disagreement among simulated players is useful before the proof stage.

## 24. Human Blind Test escalation

For SIGNATURE target:
- at least one human blind test is strongly preferred before release;
- for a flagship/expensive Premium release, treat absence of human blind test as explicit UNKNOWN unless user approves otherwise.

Testers should not receive:
- culprit;
- intended solve path;
- importance labels.

Collect:
- where they got bored;
- where they felt smart;
- where they felt cheated;
- what they believed at 25/50/75% progress;
- what they remember a day later if possible.

## 25. WOW Gate

A case may pass ordinary QA and still fail WOW Gate.

For SIGNATURE status, all must pass qualitatively:

W1. Retell Hook
The premise is compelling in one spoiler-free sentence.

W2. Signature Action
There is at least one memorable thing the player DOES, not merely watches.

W3. Earned Reversal
At least one major interpretation changes because of earlier evidence.

W4. Causal Compression
The final model explains several oddities at once.

W5. Human Truth
At least one important lie/secret feels emotionally or socially believable beyond puzzle mechanics.

W6. Fair Surprise
The player could have seen it, but reasonably may not have.

W7. Post-Reveal Inevitability
After explanation, the solution feels stronger, not more arbitrary.

W8. Product Identity
The experience feels like Mystery Logic, not a generic printable detective.

W9. Memory Residue
There is a specific image, contradiction, action or moral tension likely to remain memorable.

W10. Desire for Next Case
The ending preserves satisfaction while creating appetite for another investigation.

FAIL / UNKNOWN / NOT RUN on a critical WOW dimension => not SIGNATURE.

No arithmetic average can override a critical failure.

## 26. Anti-mediocrity vetoes

Reject or rework even if logically valid when:

- premise can be swapped with ten generic detective stories with no loss;
- culprit is simply "person with strongest motive";
- red herrings are disposable;
- solution relies on one clever timestamp;
- case is mostly reading;
- climax is a confession monologue;
- final proof depends on one obscure technical fact;
- every clue points in the same direction;
- "twist" is only hidden identity;
- characters exist only as suspects;
- setting has no causal role;
- evidence variety is cosmetic;
- there is no memorable investigative action;
- post-case explanation is much more interesting than actual play.

## 27. Mechanic × Mystery integration

A mechanic is justified only if removing it weakens the mystery.

Examples:
- Partner asymmetry changes what each player can infer;
- AI interrogation changes evidence access and behavior;
- map matters because spatial causality matters;
- audio matters because sound/timing/voice is evidence;
- document provenance matters because authenticity is disputed.

Do not add mechanics as decoration.

## 28. Premium shelf diversity

The Case Architect optimizes the catalogue as a portfolio.

Avoid releasing several adjacent cases with the same:
- setting;
- victim/crime type;
- decisive clue type;
- mechanic;
- emotional tone;
- culprit relationship;
- final reconstruction structure.

Track a diversity matrix across:
- mode;
- duration;
- difficulty;
- setting;
- incident;
- emotional tone;
- evidence mix;
- core deduction type;
- signature mechanic;
- culprit/motive archetype;
- social world.

## 29. Pattern memory and failure archive

Maintain a spoiler-safe public pattern log and private detailed notes.

Record:
- concepts rejected and why;
- logic defects found repeatedly;
- red herrings that felt artificial;
- clue types players miss too often;
- mechanics that create confusion;
- structures that produce strong aha moments;
- competitor concepts to avoid.

Do not blindly repeat successful patterns.
Use memory to avoid accidental self-cloning.

## 30. Production analytics feedback loop

After LIVE release, Case Architect may consume trustworthy analytics.

Possible signals:
- start rate;
- first meaningful action;
- evidence opens;
- hypothesis/checkpoint failures;
- hint usage;
- stage drop-off;
- completion;
- reconstruction quality where measurable;
- replay/return;
- qualitative feedback.

Rules:
- exclude QA/CI/test where possible;
- do not infer causation from event counts alone;
- do not optimize one case into sameness with all others;
- use analytics to detect friction, not to remove desirable difficulty.

After enough real data, update:
- difficulty calibration;
- hint design;
- pacing assumptions;
- clue salience patterns.

## 31. Flagship selection

Not every Premium case should be SIGNATURE.

Use three catalogue roles:
- SOLID: reliable Premium variety;
- FLAGSHIP: strongest showcase of one format;
- EXPERIMENTAL: tests a new mechanic/structure.

Case Architect should know the intended role before over-investing production effort.

A FLAGSHIP candidate must target SIGNATURE status.

## 32. Default concept-selection rule

When the user asks for the next important Premium case:

1. inspect portfolio gap;
2. refresh competitors;
3. generate 10–12 divergent seeds privately;
4. eliminate duplicates/clichés/similarity risks;
5. deepen top 3–4;
6. run Signature Promise + feasibility + portfolio fit;
7. show user only the survivors.

Do NOT make the user review ten mediocre ideas.

## 33. Default CANON rule

After concept approval:

- construct CANON privately;
- attack it before writing polished content;
- do not protect the original idea;
- if the best fix changes the culprit/mechanism, change it;
- preserve only what improves the player experience.

Author attachment is not a quality criterion.

## 34. Final author question

Before Content Freeze, answer:

"If a player paid for this case and played it tonight, what exact moment would make them tell another person tomorrow that Mystery Logic was different?"

If the answer is vague, the case is not yet a flagship.


## 35. FIRST SCREEN CLARITY / HOOK GATE — HARD RULE

This rule is mandatory for EVERY Mystery Logic case, not optional polish.

### Core principle

The player must never have to decode the product, world or terminology before they understand the incident.

The first screen must make the situation emotionally and practically clear before introducing complexity.

Within roughly 15–20 seconds, a new player must be able to answer in ordinary language:

1. WHO AM I?
What role am I playing in this investigation?

2. WHAT HAPPENED?
What concrete event/problem occurred?

3. WHY DOES IT MATTER?
What is at stake and why should I care?

4. WHAT DO I DO NOW?
What is my first investigative action?

If any answer is unclear, FIRST SCREEN = FAIL.

### No-jargon rule

The first screen MUST NOT contain unexplained:
- product codes;
- acronyms;
- internal case IDs;
- model names;
- logistics/forensic/legal/medical/technical terminology;
- fictional company vocabulary that the player must infer;
- abstract phrases such as "asymmetric evidence", "manual workflow", "serialized module", "identity chain" unless they are immediately translated into ordinary human language.

Plain-language meaning comes FIRST.
Technical label may appear SECOND, only when it becomes useful.

BAD:
"12 Vektor-M4 modules in standardized frames."

GOOD:
"12 expensive industrial measuring devices — equipment factories use to precisely test and calibrate machinery. Their model name is Vektor-M4."

If a term can be misunderstood as a weapon, headset, software product, medical device or anything else, the text MUST explicitly state what physical thing it is and what it is used for.

### Maximum semantic disclosure, minimum solution disclosure

"Do not spoil the mystery" is NOT permission to hide the premise.

Reveal the situation fully.
Hide the solution, not the basic meaning.

The player may be uncertain about:
- who did it;
- how it was done;
- which evidence matters;
- which witness lies.

The player must NOT be uncertain about:
- what object is missing;
- what happened at the scene;
- their role;
- what the visible problem is;
- why the investigation exists.

### Hook before rules

Preferred opening order:

INCIDENT / HUMAN SCENE
→ clear consequence/stakes
→ player role
→ first action
→ only then rules, systems, codes and technical context.

Do NOT lead with:
- feature chips;
- product taxonomy;
- "Premium Partner";
- CASE IDs;
- instruction lists;
- database-like fact dumps.

The player should enter a story/problem before they enter an interface.

### First-screen vocabulary test

For every noun on the first screen ask:
"Would a normal adult outside this profession understand exactly what physical thing this is?"

If NO:
- replace it with plain language;
- or immediately explain it in 3–10 simple words.

### One-screen comprehension test

Show only the first screen to a cold reader.

Ask:
- "Что произошло?"
- "Что пропало/сломалось/случилось?"
- "Почему это важно?"
- "Кто вы здесь?"
- "Что вам надо сделать?"

Any wrong, vague or hesitant answer => FAIL and rewrite.

Do NOT explain the screen verbally after the test.
If explanation is required, the screen failed.

### Hook quality

A technically clear screen can still be boring.

The first 30–60 seconds must contain:
- a concrete image/action/change;
- a contradiction or unresolved problem;
- a consequence/stake;
- an immediate investigative promise.

For flagship Premium, "read the report" is not an acceptable first action.

### Progressive complexity

Complexity is earned in layers:

Layer 0 — human situation.
Layer 1 — simple evidence.
Layer 2 — named systems/processes.
Layer 3 — specialist detail only when it enables deduction.

Never start at Layer 2 or 3.

### Automatic vetoes

FIRST SCREEN FAIL if:
- user asks "что это вообще?";
- user asks what a central object/term means;
- opening reads like a logistics/forensics/business report;
- player sees more product/system terminology than story;
- player understands mechanics but not why they should care;
- first screen could make a reasonable user close the page before taking one investigative action.

FIRST SCREEN FAIL blocks PREMIUM and SIGNATURE status regardless of logic/WOW scores.

### Chronic-error prevention

Case Architect must explicitly run this gate BEFORE:
- visual design;
- blind test;
- implementation preview;
- Content Freeze.

A later beautiful UI does not rescue a confusing first screen.


## 36. AUTONOMOUS PRE-REVIEW GATE — USER IS NOT BASIC QA

This rule is mandatory.

The user is NOT responsible for finding obvious quality defects in drafts, previews or demos.

Before showing any review build to the user, Case Architect must independently verify at minimum:

### Technical sanity
- primary CTA works in the actual delivery mode;
- page opens in target browser context;
- no blocking JavaScript errors;
- all expected evidence/stages render;
- reset/resume behavior works where relevant;
- desktop and mobile layouts render without overlap or broken controls.

### First-screen quality
- First Screen Clarity / Hook Gate passed internally;
- no unexplained jargon;
- incident, role, stakes and first action are obvious;
- opening is emotionally engaging enough to justify continuing.

### Visual quality
- no repeated hero imagery across adjacent screens unless narratively intentional;
- typography is readable at normal browser zoom;
- no body copy below reasonable game-reading size;
- primary content hierarchy is obvious;
- central screen area carries meaningful action/evidence, not empty decorative space;
- evidence looks like game material, not admin/debug UI;
- layout feels premium enough for the intended paid tier.

### UX quality
- user can understand what to do next without author explanation;
- interaction labels are human-facing, not internal project vocabulary;
- the virtual/real partner feels like part of the investigation, not a utility widget;
- no stage leaks future terminology or solution logic;
- no duplicated information that makes the experience feel padded.

### Content sanity
- player-facing text matches current CANON;
- obsolete v1 content is not accidentally mixed into active v2;
- no answer-changing fact appears only after the final accusation;
- no accidental spoilers in labels, prompts, metadata or filenames visible to players.

### Review threshold
Do NOT show the build to the user if any of the above has a known defect.

The user should be asked to judge only higher-level questions such as:
- is this compelling?
- does this feel premium?
- is the mystery enjoyable?
- does the visual direction fit the brand?
- does the experience deserve to ship/paywall?

The user should NOT have to report:
- button does not work;
- text is too small;
- opening is incomprehensible;
- same image repeats immediately;
- layout breaks;
- obvious jargon;
- raw/admin-like visual quality.

Any such user-found issue counts as AUTONOMOUS PRE-REVIEW GATE FAIL and must be recorded as a process failure, not merely a design tweak.

### Escalation rule
After one such process failure, the next review build must receive a stricter internal pass before user delivery:
- desktop render review;
- mobile render review;
- interaction smoke test;
- text-size/readability pass;
- first-screen cold-read test;
- visual repetition check;
- user-path rehearsal from start through at least first meaningful deduction.

The user approves direction and release quality; the agent owns basic craft and QA.


## 37. PROGRESSIVE VOCABULARY + NON-LEADING INVESTIGATION GATE — HARD RULE

This rule is mandatory for all Mystery Logic cases and previews.

### A. Plain meaning before codes

Player-facing evidence must present the human meaning first.

Do NOT lead with:
- container IDs;
- seal IDs;
- WMS/service job IDs;
- internal user IDs;
- unexplained abbreviations;
- English operational labels;
- technical status strings such as OCR OK / UNKNOWN / MANUAL MODE.

If an exact code is genuinely evidentiary, reveal it only when the player already understands:
1. what object/process it belongs to;
2. why the exact identifier matters;
3. what comparison the player could make with it.

Do not make players memorize identifiers that do not enable a deduction.

### B. Technical data is second-layer evidence

Use progressive disclosure:

plain-language fact
→ source/context
→ exact technical details only if needed.

BAD:
QZMU 621508 4 / HX-481920 / OCR OK

GOOD:
Камера на въезде зафиксировала тот же контейнер. Двери закрыты, пломба на месте, её номер совпадает с документами.

Exact serials may be available in a secondary detail view if later comparison truly depends on them.

### C. Every visual must explain itself

An evidence visual must let a cold player answer:
- what physical thing am I looking at?
- where/when was it recorded?
- why does this source exist?

Abstract dark rectangles, fake terminal decoration, ornamental scanlines or "tech-looking" placeholders are NOT evidence.

If the user asks "что это за картинка?", VISUAL EVIDENCE = FAIL.

### D. Interface must not teach the deduction

Normal-play UI must NOT tell the player:
- what two facts to compare;
- which suspect to ask about;
- which timeline to build;
- that there are "two chains" before the player earns that interpretation;
- where a contradiction is;
- which evidence is physical vs digital;
- which theory is strongest.

Forbidden examples:
- "Начните не с догадки, а с маршрута"
- "Сравните эти фотографии"
- "Что по Антону?"
- "Постройте отдельно путь контейнера и путь груза"
- "Где вы начинаете верить записи вместо физического факта?"

These may exist only as explicit hints requested by the player, with hint strength recorded.

### E. Mechanics may organize, not interpret

A shared board may let players:
- place facts;
- link facts;
- move cards;
- annotate;
- compare sources.

But the board must not pre-label the intended causal distinction.

Prefer:
Ваши материалы / Материалы партнёра

over:
Физический контейнер / Настоящий груз

until the player has independently discovered that distinction.

### F. Virtual partner must not become the author's mouthpiece

A virtual partner:
- knows only its current role materials;
- may have uncertain or even wrong hypotheses;
- should not always select the decisive clue;
- should not dump all evidence on a generic "что у тебя есть?";
- should not name the culprit before evidence supports it;
- should not instruct the player which materials to compare.

If the partner's advice materially reveals the intended solve path without being asked for a hint:
NON-LEADING GATE = FAIL.

### G. Player-language audit

Before every user preview, scan all visible strings for:
- internal codes;
- acronyms;
- untranslated English;
- author instructions disguised as UI copy;
- suspect names in quick-action buttons;
- "compare/check/build" prompts that reveal reasoning method.

Any avoidable instance => revise before showing the user.


## 38. PREMIUM EVIDENCE GRAPHICS ONLY — HARD BAN ON PRIMITIVE SVG/PLACEHOLDERS

This rule is mandatory for all paid Mystery Logic cases and all user-facing review builds.

### Absolute ban

Do NOT use:
- primitive SVG illustrations as evidence;
- icon-style drawings presented as photos/documents;
- fake CCTV made from simple vector boxes;
- generic route diagrams with arrows standing in for a real visual source;
- decorative "tech panels" that look like placeholders;
- repeated stock-looking pseudo-evidence;
- simple line-art containers, trucks, seals or documents as substitutes for real-looking artifacts.

These may be acceptable only for neutral navigation icons, never for player evidence.

### Premium evidence standard

If an artifact represents:
- a photo;
- CCTV frame;
- seal close-up;
- warehouse image;
- vehicle image;
- port image;
- document;
- message;
- map/route;
- inspection form;
- evidence board item;

then it must look like a production-grade artifact appropriate to the world of the case.

Preferred evidence treatments:
- realistic photographic imagery;
- high-quality generated or commissioned scene photography;
- authentic-looking document design;
- real-world style screenshots/records with restrained UI;
- cinematic evidence crops;
- layered annotations that preserve realism.

### Evidence must feel diegetic

The player should feel:
"Это реальный материал дела."

Not:
"Это нарисованная иллюстрация для объяснения."

### Visual quality gate

Before user review, inspect every evidence visual.

FAIL if any item:
- looks like a placeholder;
- looks cheaper than the approved visual direction;
- uses simplistic vector geometry as the main evidence image;
- breaks immersion;
- repeats another image without narrative reason;
- requires the player to imagine what the image is supposed to represent.

### Paid-tier rule

For Premium / FLAGSHIP / SIGNATURE:
one obviously cheap evidence graphic is enough to fail the visual gate.

Do not show the build to the user until all currently visible evidence visuals meet the premium bar.

Navigation icons may remain simple.
Evidence may not.


## 39. PLAYER AGENCY GATE — INVESTIGATION, NOT DOSSIER READING

Mandatory for Premium / FLAGSHIP / SIGNATURE cases.

### Core rule

The player must progress because they INVESTIGATE, not because they finish reading a predetermined packet.

The default loop must be:

NOTICE SOMETHING
→ FORM A QUESTION
→ CHOOSE HOW TO CHECK IT
→ REQUEST / INTERROGATE / DELEGATE / PRESENT EVIDENCE
→ RECEIVE A CANON-BOUNDED RESULT
→ UPDATE OR DEFEND A HYPOTHESIS.

A case that mainly runs:
open card → read card → click next
is an INTERACTIVE DOSSIER, not a Mystery Logic premium investigation.

### Progression

Do NOT unlock the next major stage merely because every visible evidence card was opened.

Stage progression should normally require:
- one or more player-chosen investigative actions;
- a recorded working hypothesis / reconstruction fragment;
- where appropriate, interaction with another person or partner.

The hypothesis may be wrong.
The game should reward testable reasoning, not guessing the author's answer.

### Investigative actions

Use actions such as:
- request a camera record;
- request route/GPS/access logs;
- request an earlier/later photograph;
- summon/interview a witness;
- ask a free-form question;
- present a discovered evidence item during an interview;
- delegate a concrete task to the Partner;
- trace a physical object/person/vehicle;
- request a follow-up document because of a player suspicion.

Actions must emerge from the mystery world, not look like quiz buttons.

### Interrogation

For cases positioned around AI investigation, at least some key conversations must support free-form player questions.

Canonical response pipeline:
free question
→ intent/topic
→ character knowledge/state
→ allowed answer
→ possible stress/trust/disclosure change.

Characters may:
- not know;
- misunderstand;
- evade;
- lie within canon;
- change what they disclose after evidence is presented.

They may NOT invent canon.

A pre-written transcript alone does not satisfy AI investigation differentiation.

### Evidence presentation

Where a witness statement can be challenged, allow the player to present an already discovered evidence item.

The evidence should change the permissible response, not just decorate the chat.

### Partner agency

Partner is not a clue dispenser.

The player should be able to delegate meaningful tasks:
"check the warehouse loading"
"talk to the forklift operator"
"find who changed the schedule"
etc.

Partner returns only what their role could legitimately obtain.

### Passive-reading limit

As a design target, do not allow more than roughly 60–90 seconds of passive reading without offering a meaningful investigative action.

Premium can contain rich documents.
It cannot make reading them the primary verb.

### Board / linking mechanics

Evidence boards and connection tools are optional thinking aids.
They must NOT be the main progression gate merely because the player clicked two cards together.

The board can help the player externalize thought.
It must not substitute for investigation.

### Agency audit

Before user review, ask:
- What can the player decide to investigate first?
- Can they ask a question the author did not pre-write as a button?
- Can they pursue a plausible wrong theory?
- Does their chosen action determine which evidence appears next?
- Can they delegate a concrete task?
- Do they ever have to state what they currently believe?
- Can they challenge a statement with evidence?

If the answer is mostly no:
PLAYER AGENCY GATE = FAIL.

### Flagship standard

For a flagship Partner Premium, the first meaningful investigative choice must happen within the first few minutes, not after a long evidence intake phase.
